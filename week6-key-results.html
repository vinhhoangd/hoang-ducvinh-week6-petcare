Week 6 key results: Warwick town vs Monroe town

The main question: Should the client open a dog daycare with grooming, as the first step toward a full-service pet-care center, in Warwick town or Monroe town, and proceed now or pilot first?

The answer to the client's question

Pilot first, in both towns. Don’t choose a town or sign a lease yet.

Public data can’t answer the question that decides it: how many dog owners in each town would pay for daycare and grooming. Here’s what we found:

  • Income: Warwick $117,680 ± $8,605, Monroe $116,019 ± $13,663. The ranges overlap, so the towns can’t be ranked.
  • Pet owners: no public dataset counts pets or pet spending, so demand is unknown.
  • Competitors: 24 pet-care businesses with employees in all of Orange County, with no split by town.
  • Population: Warwick has more people (31,909 vs 21,299), but people aren’t the same as dog owners.
  • Model: its error is $16,581 per tract, about ten times the income gap between the towns.

The four-week pilot:

  1. Ask both town clerks how many dogs are licensed.
  2. Put up a pre-registration page with a small refundable deposit, advertised equally in both towns.
  3. Run one pop-up daycare-and-grooming Saturday in each town.
  4. Survey local groomers about waitlists and prices.

After four weeks, open in the town with clearly more paid deposits and repeat bookings. If neither town reaches the client’s break-even number of regular customers, don’t open. If the towns are too close to call, keep testing rather than guess.

What the ledger statuses mean

Each claim in evidence/claim-ledger.csv gets one status after we open its source. Of our 14 claims: 7 supported, 3 proxy, 2 unsupported, 2 refuted. No row is ever deleted, even when it turns out wrong.

StatusMeaningOur example
supportedThe source directly shows the claim is true.L4: Warwick $117,680 ± $8,605 and Monroe $116,019 ± $13,663 really do overlap.
proxyThe number is real, but it only stands in for what the client wants to know.L6: Warwick has more residents (31,909 vs 21,299), but residents aren’t dog owners.
unsupportedThe source doesn’t say either way. The claim might be true, but nothing we checked backs it up.L5: the CBP page never defines pet care (812910) vs vets (541940). It’s silent, not wrong.
refutedThe source shows the claim is false, or a number is presented as fact with no source at all.C1: “ACS shows Warwick has roughly 4,491 pet-owning households.” The ACS doesn’t count pets, so the number was invented.

The difference instructors test most: unsupported means we couldn’t confirm it; refuted means we showed it’s wrong. In Step 8 we moved L5 from refuted to unsupported for exactly that reason, and moved L2 and L3 up to supported after opening their sources.

The leakage test (F3)

The course gave us a table with one extra column, income_band. We predicted the forest would score about 0.5 on held-out tracts, wrote that down, and then ran model/leakage_test.py (86 tracts, 64 train / 22 held out, seed 42).

RunTrain R²Held-out R²Held-out MAETop features
With every column0.9960.989$3,127income_band 0.969, renter share 0.012, population 0.004
Without income_band0.9330.781$12,996bachelor’s or higher 0.452, renter share 0.211, commute 30+ min 0.081
  • income_band equals median income ÷ 10,000 rounded down in 86 of 86 rows. It’s the answer in disguise.
  • Its 16 bands never overlap, and its rank correlation with income is 0.995.
  • A near-perfect 0.989 looked impressive and meant nothing. In real use you wouldn’t know a tract’s income band without already knowing its income.
  • This table is the course’s leakage sample, which is why its scores differ from our own model on 91 tracts (0.552).

What the data can and can’t tell the client

Can tell

  • The two towns can’t be told apart on household income: the margins of error overlap.
  • Warwick has about half again as many residents (31,909 vs 21,299), a bigger pool of possible customers.
  • The county has 24 pet-care businesses with employees (2022), so the trade exists locally.

Can’t tell

  • How many households own a dog, or what they spend on pet care.
  • Who the competitors are near a specific address: the business count covers the whole county and leaves out one-person businesses.
  • Which town’s households earn more.
  • Local prices for grooming or daycare: the FRED series is a national pet-food index in index points, not dollars.

Two of the numbers have known limits: the Census figures are survey estimates averaged over 2019–2023, and each town is only a few tracts (Warwick 8, Monroe 5), so small differences can be noise.
